## In one sentence

A nematode is a tiny soil creature that **attacks the roots** of plants.

## Why it matters

One control measure is **rotation or succession** with plants in which the nematode multiplies little. In a study published in Revista Caatinga, *Crotalaria juncea*, *C. spectabilis* and pigeon pea IPR 43 were poor hosts of two root lesion nematodes.

In a field study in Mato Grosso (Debiasi and colleagues), crotalarias lowered the root lesion nematode before and early in the soybean season; at the soybean harvest, the difference was gone. With corn and with brachiaria grasses, the population stayed the same as in the comparison area.

For the soybean cyst nematode, Embrapa says that a non-host plant in the off-season alone is not enough: summer rotation is needed.

## In your field

You can tell the app whether there are nematodes in the area. If you say yes, the app changes the math: pest control counts more in the soil score, and the [3-year plan](/plano) takes that into account.

See [crotalaria](/guia/crotalaria), one of the plants in the studies.

Which nematode species you have and which resistant variety to use are up to your agronomist.
