## In one sentence

Erosion is rain carrying away the good top layer of the soil, and it is worse when the soil is bare.

## Why it matters

A raindrop hitting bare soil breaks the soil apart. The water running off carries that soil downhill and cuts channels. Fertilizer and organic matter go with it.

Three practices work together:

- **Cover:** straw protects the soil from the impact of the drops and helps water soak into the soil.
- **No-till:** planting without plowing or harrowing keeps the straw on top of the soil.
- **Contour lines and terraces:** planting and working the soil across the slope, not straight down the hill, holds the water. A terrace keeps the water of a heavy rain inside the field.

No-till alone is not enough. With little straw, no rotation and compacted soil, erosion comes back. That is why terraces are still useful together with no-till.

## In your field

Right after the soybean harvest, in February and March, the soil may be bare in the season of heavy rains. On the [What the satellite saw](/satelite) screen, the heavy rain alert says how many days a year it rained a lot in that season, from NASA's GPM IMERG satellite.

Planting right after the harvest covers the soil sooner. See in the [Suggestion](/resposta) and in [Compare](/opcoes) how much straw each option leaves, and read [Straw: why cover the soil](/guia/palhada).

Where to build terraces and how far apart they should be is up to your agronomist.
