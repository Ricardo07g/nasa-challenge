## In one sentence

Erosion is rain **carrying away the good top layer of the soil**, and it is worse when the soil is bare.

## Why it matters

A raindrop hitting bare soil knocks soil grains loose, and runoff carries them away. Two practices work together:

- **Cover:** straw on the soil is the most important factor in absorbing the impact of the drops and keeps the soil from breaking loose.
- **Terraces:** in a study in southern Brazil, in a no-till field, terraces cut soil loss by **58.7%**, mainly by holding back runoff. Another study found more water for plants in the terraced area during dry periods.

No-till alone is not enough when runoff is heavy: research recommends combining terraces with plenty of straw.

## In your field

Right after the soybean harvest, the soil may be bare. On the [What the satellite saw](/satelite) screen, the heavy rain alert shows how many days a year it rained a lot, from NASA's GPM IMERG satellite.

See the cover options in the [Suggestion](/resposta) and in [Compare](/opcoes), and read [Straw: why cover the soil](/guia/palhada).

Where to build terraces and how far apart they should be is up to your agronomist.
