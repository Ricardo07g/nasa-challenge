## In one sentence

After the soybean harvest, the rain fades until it stops, and knowing **when it usually ends** helps you choose what to plant.

## Why it matters

The plant that comes after soybeans lives on the rain that is still to fall and the water the soil stores. If the rain ends before the phase when the plant needs water most, water runs short right then.

Every day of delay in the harvest is one less day of rain for the next plant.

## In your field

On the [When you harvest](/quando) screen, the app shows three things, with rain measured by the GPM IMERG satellite over the last 20 years:

1. **The rain that is left** from harvest day to June 30. One figure is for a normal year (the median). The other is for a dry year: only 20% of years had less rain than that.
2. **When the rain ends.** The app calls the end of the rain the first day, from March 1, that starts 20 days in a row with less than 10 mm in total. It shows the day this happened in half of the years, the earliest year and the latest.
3. **Month by month**, the rain and what the crop uses. What it uses is evapotranspiration, calculated with NASA POWER climate. The month when rain falls below use is written out in highlight.

The numbers March 1, 20 days, 10 mm and 20% are a team hypothesis.
