## In one sentence

In no-till, the seed goes into the soil without plowing or harrowing, under the straw of the previous crop.

## Why it matters

Soil that is not turned keeps the straw on top. The straw protects the soil from the impact of rain and from heat and helps hold moisture. Over time, it becomes organic matter and makes the soil firmer against erosion.

No-till rests on two pillars:

- **Straw all year:** the soil needs cover after every harvest.
- **Crop rotation:** changing the plants helps control pests, diseases, weeds and nematodes.

Without enough straw and without rotation, the system loses strength.

## In your field

The off-season after soybeans is the time to grow the straw for the next season. Cover crops such as [pearl millet](/guia/milheto) and [corn with brachiaria](/guia/milho-braquiaria) leave a lot of straw. Leaving the soil fallow leaves nothing.

The app shows how much straw each option leaves in the Soil line of the [Suggestion](/resposta) and of [Compare](/opcoes). The [3-year plan](/plano) helps you build the rotation. Also read [Straw: why cover the soil](/guia/palhada), [Crop rotation](/guia/rotacao-de-culturas) and [How to prevent erosion](/guia/como-evitar-erosao).

How to dry down the cover before planting and which machine to use are up to your agronomist.
