## In one sentence

In no-till planting, the seed goes into the soil **without plowing or disking**, under the straw of the previous crop.

## Why it matters

Soil that is not turned over keeps the straw on top. The straw protects the soil from the impact of rain and from heat, and helps hold moisture. Over time, it becomes organic matter and makes the soil firmer against erosion.

No-till rests on two pillars:

- **Straw all year:** the soil needs cover after every harvest.
- **Crop rotation:** changing the plants helps control pests, diseases, weeds and nematodes.

Without enough straw and without rotation, the system loses strength.

## In your field

The off-season after soybeans is the time to produce the straw for the next crop. Cover plants, like [pearl millet](/guia/milheto), and [corn with brachiaria](/guia/milho-braquiaria) leave a lot of straw. Leaving the soil fallow leaves none.

The app shows how much straw each option leaves in the Soil row of the [Suggestion](/resposta) and of [Compare](/opcoes). The [3-year plan](/plano) helps build the rotation. Also read [Straw: why cover the soil](/guia/palhada), [Crop rotation](/guia/rotacao-de-culturas) and [How to prevent erosion](/guia/como-evitar-erosao).

How to dry down the cover before planting and which machine to use are up to your agronomist.
