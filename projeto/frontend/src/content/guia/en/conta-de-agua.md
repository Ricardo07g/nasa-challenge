## In one sentence

The app treats the soil as a **bucket**: rain fills it, the plant empties it, and what is missing is thirst.

## Why it matters

What decides the second crop is not just the year's rain. It is how much water the soil holds and how much the plant uses each day. Climate risk zoning for corn uses the same idea: the crop's water balance, run for each soil type according to how much water it holds.

## In your field

Every day, the app does the math:

1. **Rain comes in**, seen by the GPM IMERG satellite.
2. **The plant uses water.** The use comes from heat and sun (NASA POWER) and from the crop.
3. **The bucket size** comes from the soil: clay and sand, from the SoilGrids map.
4. **The starting point** can be the moisture measured by the SMAP satellite, when there is any.

When the bucket runs dry, water is short. See [What "it worked" means](/guia/deu-certo).

In no-till, straw helps conserve soil moisture. See [straw](/guia/palhada).

In the [3-year plan](/plano), "rain used" shows this for each year. The [Suggestion](/resposta) has the Water row.
