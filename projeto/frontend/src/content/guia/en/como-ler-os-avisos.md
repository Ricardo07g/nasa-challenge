## In one sentence

The Alerts screen puts together what satellites, the El Niño forecast and your neighbors show about the point you marked.

## Why it matters

The alerts help you get ready, but they are not official alerts. The app sends no messages: the alerts show up when you open the app, for the field saved on your device.

Each alert gives the level (**Warning** or **Keep an eye on it**), what the data shows, what to do, the source and the date of the reading. A reading older than 15 days is marked as old.

## In your field

On the [Alerts](/avisos) screen, the alerts come in four groups:

- **Now**: dry soil from the SMAP satellite; days in a row without rain and 35 °C heat in the last days, from NASA POWER; and the NOAA El Niño or La Niña forecast, with what happened here in similar years.
- **What changed in your area**: 2020 to 2024 compared with 2005 to 2019. These are few years: it shows what happened, not a forecast.
- **Out of the ordinary**: the records of the 20 years, such as the longest dry spell and the driest autumn.
- **Near you**: what neighbors reported in the last 7 days. It is a report, not confirmed.

For the official alerts, see [Official Civil Defense alerts](/guia/alertas-defesa-civil). See also [Dry spell](/guia/veranico) and [El Niño and La Niña](/guia/el-nino-la-nina).
