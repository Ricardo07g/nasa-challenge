# Summary

*NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 form field, plain text, 1,500-character limit (this text: 1182).*

Every year between January and March, soybean farmers in Brazil's Cerrado must decide what to plant after the harvest, just as the rains begin to end. Most repeat second-crop corn or leave the field bare, which degrades the soil and exposes farms to drought and heat losses, with almost no crop insurance.

Raiz Orbital is a responsive web app that answers one question for a specific field: "What should I plant after soybeans?" The farmer marks the field and the harvest date. Raiz Orbital replays 20 seasons of NASA observations (GPM IMERG, NASA POWER, SMAP, MODIS) and NASA climate projections for 2041–2060 (NEX-GDDP-CMIP6), combined with local soil data, to test six off-season options. It delivers a detailed X-ray of the land, one recommendation, a 3-year rotation plan with a Plan B for dry autumns, alerts, a printable report and a bilingual guide. Every number shows its source.

This is exactly what Field Shift asks for: NASA Earth observations, local soil, crop traits and farmer priorities turned into rotation strategies that strengthen soil health and adapt farms to a changing climate. Built in 48 hours in Uberlândia, Brazil. Eyes on the sky, roots in the ground.
